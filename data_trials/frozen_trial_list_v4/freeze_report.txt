THE LIST
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  trials by type   {'fog_only': 60, 'sat_only': 60, 'conflict': 30, 'together': 30, 'consistency': 15, 'catch': 10, 'practice': 6}
  scenes           47, at most 7 analysed trials from one
  r(d_sat, d_fog)  -0.115  -- what decides
                   whether the two weights can be told
                   apart; above about 0.6 they cannot

  THE CATCH TRIAL, mode 'no_images'. 10 trials. The
  registered rule: EXCLUDE A PARTICIPANT FAILING 3 OR MORE.

  What a catch trial is in this mode: no pictures. A line
  of text names one arrow key -- UP or DOWN -- and the
  trial takes one press. The key sequence and the wording
  live in the experiment, not in this list; the list
  carries only catch_mode, so the experiment can refuse to
  run a list built for the other mode.

  THE KEY-PRESS ASSUMPTION behind the threshold. The
  response set has four live keys: left and right for the
  image choice, up and down for the catch trials. A
  participant who is not reading presses one of the four
  at random, so three presses in four are wrong and the
  per-trial failure probability is 0.75 * lambda, where
  lambda is the lapse rate. That assumption is what makes
  the numbers below, and it is stated because it is an
  assumption: a participant who always presses the same
  key, or who reads the instruction but mis-aims, is not
  this model.

  At 3 or more failures out of 10, binomial:
    lambda 0.00   excluded 0.0%    an attentive
                                   participant is never
                                   excluded by chance
    lambda 0.05   excluded 0.5%
    lambda 0.10   excluded 3.4%
    lambda 0.20   excluded 18.0%
    lambda 0.30   excluded 39.8%
    lambda 0.50   excluded 78.9%
    random presser    99.96%   (p_fail 0.75)
    left/right only  100.00%   (p_fail 1.00: an up or
                               down trial is never
                               answered)

  So the rule catches gross inattention almost always and
  costs almost nothing at the attentive end. It is NOT a
  filter on mild inattention: a participant lapsing on one
  trial in ten survives it 97% of the time, which is why
  the lapse rate is also fitted, as the pre-registered
  sensitivity analysis for C1, rather than screened for
  here.

  IN 'images' MODE the catch trial shows a pair with an
  obvious difference and takes a left/right press, so a
  random press is wrong only half the time: p_fail is
  0.5 * lambda, and the same threshold excludes 1.2% at
  lambda 0.1 and 7.0% at lambda 0.2. The rule is kept at 3
  because it is the registered one; the mode is recorded
  so the arithmetic that applies is never in doubt.

  SESSION LENGTH. 211 trials presented (180 analysed,
  15 consistency, 10 catch, 6 practice). At 3.5 to 4.5
  seconds per trial plus about two minutes of instructions
  and breaks, that is 14 to 18 minutes. THE 15-MINUTE
  TARGET IS AT THE FAST END OF THAT RANGE. The per-trial
  time is the assumption to check against the first
  participants' timestamps; the counts are fixed here.
