THIS SCRIPT CHOOSES NOTHING
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  It measures and stops. The version is chosen by a person
  from the tables below.

WHY EVERY VERSION IS MEASURED OVER MANY DRAWS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  A trial list is 180 trials sampled from a pool of
  thousands. Two versions can differ because one is a
  better design or because one drew better, and a single
  draw cannot tell those apart. Step 5 therefore builds
  each version at several seeds, and every table here is a
  mean over draws with the between-draw spread beside it.

  THE FIRST RUN OF THIS SCRIPT USED ONE DRAW PER VERSION,
  and it misled. The fog control read 0.531 for `current`
  against 0.508 for `larger_diffs` at 0.50 SD, with a
  Monte Carlo error of 0.011 -- an apparently real cost of
  the colour cut. It was two different draws of fog trials,
  because step 5 drew every trial type off one random
  stream and the cut moved the stream. CHECK 4 below now
  verifies that cannot happen, and the difference columns
  use the spread between draws rather than the simulation
  error.

  The simulated people are seeded by DRAW INDEX, not by
  version. So within an index every version faces the same
  people, and differences are PAIRED by index -- the
  participant noise is common to both rates and cancels.
  The shipping draw is step 5's seed, fixed in advance; it
  is reported as one draw among the rest and no seed is
  chosen on any result here.

THE ESTIMATOR CHANGED, AND WHY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  The per-person fit is now Firth's bias-reduced logistic
  regression. It used to be plain maximum likelihood with a
  ridge of 1e-06, nominally there to keep the
  fit finite under separation -- a participant whose
  answers the model predicts perfectly, where no finite
  maximum-likelihood estimate exists.

  That ridge did nothing. The predictors were in raw
  feature units, so the diagonal it was added to ran to
  tens of thousands and the ridge was 3.6e-11 of it;
  refitting at 1e-3 moved the weights by less than 1e-5.
  The fits were maximum-likelihood in practice and
  unprotected in principle.

  Excluding separable fits was considered and rejected: a
  participant who answers every colour trial as predicted
  is the strongest evidence this design can produce, and
  dropping them would bias against the hypothesis. Firth is
  always finite, differs from the MLE by about 2% away from
  separation, and removes most of the small-sample bias --
  which matters because the registered control comparison
  reads magnitudes between groups, not just signs.

  THE FLOORS USE THE SAME ESTIMATOR. Firth shrinks the
  guessers' weights as well as the responders', so the two
  effects largely cancel in a detection rate -- 'largely'
  being why the whole comparison was re-run rather than
  argued. information_by_seed.csv carries both floors per
  draw, under floor_*_SD and floor_*_SD_other.

  THE UNITS CHANGED WITH IT. The fit now uses `pred_sat`
  and `pred_fog` as step 5 writes them, in corpus-SD units,
  so this script, the registered model and step 8 are the
  same model. Firth is equivariant under a linear change of
  predictor scale (CHECK 1e), so no estimate moved because
  of the unit change.

CHECK 4: STREAM SEPARATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  The colour cut filters colour-only pairs and nothing
  else, so two versions built from the same ladder must
  have IDENTICAL fog_only, together and conflict trials,
  at every draw.

  This was once false. `current` and `larger_diffs`, which
  differ only in the cut, shared 1 of 60 fog_only trials,
  1 of 30 together and 0 of 30 conflict. Two consequences:
  the fog condition was not a control, and colour
  information leaked in from types the cut never touches --
  `both` gained 16.4 units of summed squared colour
  predictor from its `together` trials alone, about a sixth
  of its total gain over `current`.

  Checked here as well as in step 5 because this is where
  the numbers are read, and a measurement is only as good

  costing fog detection, which is the one thing it must
  not do.

  Versions on a DIFFERENT ladder are not a control here --

8. WHAT TO DECIDE, AND WHAT THIS CANNOT TELL YOU
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  The choice is between detection and the dose-response
  range given up to get it. This script measures the first
  and describes the second; it cannot weigh them for you.

  Four things it does NOT measure:
    - whether the larger edits look artificial, which is a
      question for the contact sheets, not a simulation;
    - how a real participant differs from a logistic model
      with a fixed weight and no lapse rate;
    - anything about the grid blocks, which use different
      cells and are step 7's business;
    - whether a cross-ladder difference is the vivid levels
      or the different mixed trials they bring with them.
      Within a ladder that is settled by CHECK 4; across
      ladders it is not, and the between-draw spread is
      what keeps it honest.

  Once you choose, step 7 freezes that list: rebuild it,
  checksum trial_list.json and the package, and record the
  checksum, settings and seed in the documentation.
