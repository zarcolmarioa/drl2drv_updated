WHAT THIS ADDS TO STEP 6
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Step 6 asked which list catches a participant with a
  given sensitivity most often. It assumed a participant
  who never slips, and it only asked whether a fitted
  weight cleared a floor -- never whether the weight was
  any good. Both matter here for different reasons.

  LAPSES, because a design that wins only for a perfect
  responder is not worth registering.

  WEIGHT STABILITY, because the registered control
  comparison compares weights BETWEEN GROUPS. Detection
  needs only the sign; a group difference needs the
  magnitude to mean something. On a list of mostly large
  colour differences a sensitive participant can answer
  nearly every colour trial as predicted, and in the limit
  the data are separable, the maximum-likelihood weight is
  infinite, and what the fit returns is set by the penalty
  -- which is 1e-06, almost nothing. A handful of those
  fits inflates a group's mean and variance.

  THE SIMULATION IS PAIRED THREE WAYS. One set of uniform
  random numbers per (draw, sensitivity, participant type)
  serves every list and every lapse rate, so the same
  person with the same luck meets all four lists at all
  three lapse rates.

CHECK 2: THE FLOORS DO NOT DEPEND ON THE LAPSE RATE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  A pure guesser answers at 0.5, and mixing that with a
  coin flip is still 0.5 EXACTLY, so the null distribution
  of fitted weights cannot move with lambda. The floors
  are therefore computed once per draw and shared across
  the lapse rates -- which is an argument, so it is tested
  twice.

  2a is the exact test: the SAME uniform draws at every
  lambda, so the responses and the floors must agree to the
  last bit. This is what would catch a bug in how lambda is
  applied, and it is worth having because the same code
  generates the lapsing participants in section A.

  2b is the sampling test: a FRESH guesser set per lambda,
  as the detection loop's floors are built, judged against
  a MEASURED spread -- 12 independent sets at
  lambda 0 give the set-to-set SD, and a difference between
  two independent sets has sqrt(2) times that. An earlier
  version of this check used ONE extra set as its
  reference, which is a single draw from the distribution
  of differences rather than its spread, and understated it
  about threefold.

  WHAT THE CHANGE OF ESTIMATOR DID. Every cell was
  fitted twice on the SAME responses, with firth
  and with the plain maximum-likelihood estimator the
  earlier version of this script used. Paired within a
  draw, so a shift is a within-draw difference.
    largest shift in the mean weight 0.0240 SD (2.3%)
    largest shift in its SD          0.0061 SD
  Firth shrinks towards zero, so a small negative shift
  is the bias correction working rather than a
  discrepancy. Section C has it per cell, with the
  floors and the detection rates beside it.

  WHY THE ESTIMATOR CHANGED. The old fits used a ridge
  of 1e-06 on predictors in RAW feature units, so the
  diagonal of X'WX ran to tens of thousands and the
  penalty sat eleven orders of magnitude below it --
  see penalty_history.csv. Those fits were therefore
  maximum-likelihood in practice, and gave no finite
  estimate for a participant the model predicted
  perfectly. The only options were to exclude that
  participant or to report a number the data did not
  support, and excluding is the worse of the two: a
  separable fit in the predicted direction is the
  strongest evidence for the effect, so dropping it
  biases against the hypothesis. Firth's penalty is
  always finite and keeps the participant, which is why
  it is the registered fit. The separation diagnostics
  above are still reported per cell and per draw --
  they describe the data, not the solver.

  comparison. It was not made on these numbers: Firth
  was registered because it gives a finite estimate
  for a participant the model predicts perfectly, and
  this section is the check that it changed nothing
  else.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SETTLE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Settles: whether the candidate order and the size of the
  gains survive lapses of 0.1 and 0.2, and how often a fit
  runs away on each candidate.

  Does not settle:
    - whether a real participant is a logistic responder
      with a fixed weight plus a lapse rate at all. The
      lapse model is the standard allowance for
      inattention, not a model of a person.
    - whether the larger colour edits look edited, which
      is a question for the contact sheets at step 7.
    - the power of the control comparison, which needs the
      group structure and sample size and is step 8's.

  The decision rule was fixed before this ran: `both`
  ships if the ranking and the gains hold and the weights
  are stable; otherwise sens_cut_25_5. Nothing here chose a
  seed or a version.
