1. WHAT THIS BLOCK IS FOR
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Two blocks of four screens. Each screen shows one
  feature at every level at once and asks which column
  looks most like the world looked -- during the episode
  in one block, normally in the other.

  DESCRIPTIVE ONLY. It locates an experience on the
  manipulated range; it does not test the hypothesis. The
  registered analyses in step 8 use the pair trials alone.
  The quantity of interest is the normal-to-episode shift,
  in FEATURE UNITS rather than grid steps: the contrast-up
  step is about +0.22 SD and the fog steps are -0.13 SD and
  larger, so one step is not a constant amount of anything.

2. WHY TWO MATCHED SCENE SETS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  The shift is a within-person comparison of two blocks, so
  a photograph may not appear in both: recognising it would
  anchor the second answer. 24 scenes are chosen as 12
  MATCHED PAIRS, one member to each set, in the SAME SLOT.

  Pairs rather than set means: matched means can hide a set
  that is bimodal, and a pair bound to a slot makes the two
  blocks comparable row by row. The per-pair distances are
  in pairs.csv.

  which is drawn at the FULL 512 px. The grid shows
  cells at 149-239 px, so a difference invisible at
  512 px cannot appear there, and the mock-up could
  only confirm it.

  The damage figures were never the binding
  constraint: gain 1.0875 does no measured damage at
  all on 40 of 47 scenes and is still invisible. The
  gain holds the mean fixed and moves only the
  histogram tails -- about +7 on lum_p95 and -6 on
  lum_p05 -- so the change lives in a small fraction
  of the frame and downscaling averages it away.
  Haze moves the same statistics further AND lays a
  coherent veil over every pixel, which is what
  survives a thumbnail.

6. CENSORING
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  THE FOG AXIS IS CENSORED AT THE ORIGINAL. The fog
  screen has no column clearer than the photograph as
  taken, so a participant whose vision is clearer than
  that answers on the original column.

  In the NORMAL-APPEARANCE block this is the common
  case, not the edge case: most people's normal vision
  is not foggy. Every fog answer on the original
  column is a LOWER BOUND on that person's normal, and
  the normal-to-episode shift computed from it is a
  lower bound on the shift.

  The bias is conservative -- it can only shrink the
  shift -- but it is declared rather than absorbed.
  REPORT THE COUNT of fog answers on the original
  column in EACH BLOCK, alongside the shift.

  The colour screen keeps p75 above the original, so the
  colour axis is open on that side.
