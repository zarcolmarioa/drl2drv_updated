1. WHAT IS BEING REGISTERED
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  The COUNT RULE at every N from 10 to 20. The sign test never
  overtakes it in the planning cell within that range.
  Each N's rule and its power are in combined_rule.csv;
  registration_table.csv carries both rules, the
  false-claim margin and the two cost cells.

  WITH THE TUNING COST IN THE SAME BREATH. The rule is
  calibrated in one cell. In weaker cells the sign test
  would outperform it for colour from N = 15 (section 6);
  for fog the count rule stays ahead throughout. The
  registered power figures are the planning-cell ones,
  and they are the rule's best case.

  FEWER THAN 10 ANALYSABLE PARTICIPANTS. If the end date
  arrives with fewer than 10, NEITHER EXISTENCE CLAIM IS
  MADE. The counts in each direction are reported
  descriptively, per feature, and nothing is concluded
  from them. C1 is still run: it compares two groups
  and does not depend on the per-person rule. This is
  registered in advance so that a small sample is not
  met with a decision taken after seeing it.

2. WHY THE COUNT RULE THROUGHOUT
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  A combination of the two rules was considered, and
  rejected. The case for it was that the count rule
  loses power as N grows: with m = 0 one
  opposite-direction detection vetoes the claim, every
  non-responder added is another chance of that veto,
  and so more participants can mean less power.

  THAT IS TRUE OF THE m = 0 RULE AND ONLY OF IT. The
  rule selected in the planning cell has m > 0 from
  N = 11 on, so no single opposite-direction detection
  can veto anything. The scan in section 3 is the
  evidence: the sign test does not overtake the count
  rule for either feature at any N in the registered
  range.

  THE POWER DOES NOT RISE MONOTONICALLY, and the
  registration should not claim it does. The rule is
  re-selected at every N under the false-rate
  ceiling, and where the ceiling forces a stricter m
  the power falls even though N grew:

  Power is quoted PER N from the table, so nothing
  in the registered analysis depends on the sequence
  being monotone. What is not available is the
  statement that another participant always helps:
  at the N where m tightens, one more can lower the
  power of the rule that N selects.

  The sign test stays in the table at every N as a
  ROBUSTNESS COMPARISON, not as part of the
  registered analysis. Reporting it costs nothing and
  shows that the registered choice was not the only
  one available.

4. EQUAL BEHAVIOUR: HOW EQUAL
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  A weight per SD is not comparable between the features:
  the informative colour trials are about 1.0 corpus SD
  apart and the fog trials about 0.64, so the same weight
  gives a smaller choice bias on fog. The pairs are
  therefore matched on the choice probability.

  MATCHING ON THE PROBABILITY DOES NOT MATCH THE
  PER-PERSON DETECTION RATE. The count rule is saturated
  on k at these sizes, so it barely moves with that rate;
  the sign test compares the two counts and is sensitive
  to it. That is why the higher pair can match on the
  count rule and not on the sign test. It does not
  affect what is registered: the count rule wins for
  both features at every N in both cells, so the
  imperfect match of the higher pair changes no
  selection. It is reported because the two features'
  claims are stated as equally strong, and on the
  per-person detection rate they are not: 0.80 against
  0.67 at the planning pair. That belongs in the
  preregistration as a limitation of the stimuli.

  than 0.01 are treated as TIED, because the
  simulation's own Monte Carlo error at a power near 0.93
  is about 0.006. Among the tied the SMALLER m is taken:
  the rule that tolerates fewer opposite-direction
  detections, and so is the harder of the two to satisfy
  by luck.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
  The count rule is calibrated at ONE cell: the planning
  weights at half share. A rule tuned at a cell is better
  there and can be worse away from it, so the
  planning-cell power on its own is the rule quoting its
  own best case. Two further cells are in the table for
  both rules at every N:

      lapse 0.1
    cost_planning_qtr
      the planning weights (colour 0.5/SD, fog 0.7/SD) at share 0.25,
      lapse 0.1

  Crossovers, per cell and feature:
    lower_half, colour       sign overtakes from N = 15
    lower_half, fog          the count rule wins throughout
    planning_qtr, colour     sign overtakes from N = 15
    planning_qtr, fog        the count rule wins throughout

  Where the sign test overtakes, the registered rule is
  the weaker of the two IN THAT CELL. That is the trade,
  and it belongs in the registration next to the
  planning-cell power rather than behind it: the planning
  cell is registered because it is the scenario in which
  a study of 10 to 20 participants can succeed at all,
  not because it is the only one considered.
