WHAT THE SIGN TEST GAINS AND COSTS RELATIVE TO THE COUNT RULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
GAINS. The sign test's false-claim rate does not depend on the taste
spread: under any symmetric no-effect model it is at most 0.0481 (N = 18),
and at most 0.0194 (upper bound) under the five simulated models. The
count rule holds its 5% (upper bound at most 0.0493) only at the spreads
it was calibrated against; outside them its rate reaches 0.1408 (N = 15, q
= 0.315 per direction). The sign test is a standard, published test (Dixon
& Mood, 1946); the count rule is this study's own construction, selected
per N by simulation.

COSTS. In the planning cell the sign test is less powerful than the count
rule at every N in the range: the difference (sign minus count) ranges
-0.217 to -0.010 on fog and -0.267 to -0.037 on colour. The test cannot
fire with fewer than 5 detections, and with 5 to 7 it needs every detected
participant in the predicted direction; at N = 10 in the planning cell,
P(at least 5 detections) is fog 0.46, colour 0.69. In the two cost cells:
weaker pair, half respond, fog: the count rule is more powerful at every
N; weaker pair, half respond, colour: the sign test is at least as
powerful from N = 15; planning weights, quarter respond, fog: the count
rule is more powerful at every N; planning weights, quarter respond,
colour: the sign test is at least as powerful from N = 15. Its guarantee,
like the count rule's calibration, holds for symmetric no-effect models
only: neither rule is protected against a preference shared across people
in the predicted direction.

PRIMARY AND SECONDARY TOGETHER. Each rule is calibrated on its own. With
no effect, the probability that at least one of the two claims is made
reaches 0.0522 within the calibrated models (colour, N = 18, taste 1x; 1
cell(s) above 0.05), and 0.14 over all symmetric models (colour, N = 15,
where the count rule alone reaches 0.14). The two rules are not a family
with joint control at 5%.
