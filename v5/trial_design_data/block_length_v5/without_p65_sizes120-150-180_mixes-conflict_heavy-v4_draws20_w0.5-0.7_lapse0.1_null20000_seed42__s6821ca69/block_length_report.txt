------------------------------------------------------------------------------

functions (level set 'without_p65'), and on every set (= one block list) it simulated:
  * 20,000 random choosers -> two-sided thresholds (2.5% per tail) and the
    null spread of each weight;
  * 10,000 people at the planning weights (saturation 0.5, contrast 0.7 per SD)
    at each lapse rate [0.0, 0.1] -> detection and precision;
and on each pair of sets, per scenario, 10,000 people fitted on both blocks, from which 4,000
studies of N = [10, 15, 20] were sampled -> paired-test power. Firth throughout (v4's
fit_firth_batch). Figures are the mean over draws, with the range [min-max] over
draws (and over the two sets of each draw, for per-list figures).

a responder adds a shared preference to both blocks and a shift to the target block

11. NOTES AND LIMITS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Power is a first estimate under the stated person model. The shift sizes, the taste
  spread, the share of responders and the lapse rate are assumptions, not estimates;
  section 6 shows how far the answer moves when they change.
* Each weight is tested on its own at alpha, uncorrected. A corrected or joint primary
  test would need the power read from 'both' or recomputed.
* Block order is not modelled: an order effect would add to the difference in half
  the people and subtract in the other half, widening its spread.
* Taste is assumed stable across blocks. Taste that differed between blocks would
  add its own variance to every difference.
* The session length covers the two pair blocks only and is an estimate: the response
  times and screen times are assumptions, to be replaced by a timed pilot.
