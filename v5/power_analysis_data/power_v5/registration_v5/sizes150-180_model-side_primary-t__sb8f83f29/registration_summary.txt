PLANNING ASSUMPTIONS ARE ASSUMPTIONS, NOT ESTIMATES. Every power and
false-positive rate below comes from simulated people whose preferences
(responders' shift of 0.5 saturation and 0.7 contrast per corpus SD, half of
the report group responding), taste spread, lapse rates and nuisances were
chosen for planning. No image-choice data exist yet.
Both block sizes (150 and 180 analysed trials per block) are reported; the
size is chosen after the timed pilot.

1. WHAT IS REGISTERED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * Per-person model: Firth logistic regression without intercept, fitted per
  block on the analysed trials, with a saturation weight, a contrast weight
  and a side term (+1 when image A is on the left, -1 on the right). The side
  term is a nuisance term; its estimate is never reported as a result.
  * Detection thresholds: per set and block size, the 2.5% tails of 20,000
  random choosers' weights under the registered model (table 1). They replace
  the two-weight thresholds frozen in design.json.
  * PRIMARY test (H1 contrast, H2 saturation), in the report group: one-sided
  paired t-test on the within-person difference (derealization-block weight
  minus ordinary-block weight), alpha 0.05 each, uncorrected. The exact
  Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the same differences is reported as a
  robustness check.
  * The mixed-effects logistic model was evaluated and is NOT registered
  (section 4).
  * SECONDARY: detected counts and the sign test within each block (v4's rule:
  d >= 5 and the exact one-sided binomial), with the registered thresholds.
  * C1: section 7. The comparison group's expectation block: section 8.

2. THE PER-PERSON MODEL AND ITS THRESHOLDS (script 6)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Rule fixed before the run: register the side model only if (a) a side bias
  of 0.5 logit moves the two-weight model's false-detection rate above 0.035
  in either tail or a weight by more than 0.05, and (b) the side model loses
  at most 0.01 of detection at the planning weights.
  Outcome: (a) holds: worst tail rate 0.0354 (180 B, saturation, right 0.5);
  worst weight shift 0.0565 (150 B, contrast, planning weights, right 0.5);
  the weight shift is what decides (a), the tail rate is within Monte Carlo
  error of the limit. (b) holds: largest detection loss 0.0046 (150 A,
  saturation). Chosen: side model.
  How the thresholds were computed: Both models' thresholds come from the same
  20,000 random choosers (the stream that produced design.json), so the two
  models are compared without the Monte Carlo error of two separate threshold
  draws. In script 6's first run the side model's thresholds came from
  separate choosers; the detection-loss criterion (b) then failed on threshold
  noise (0.014, with a Monte Carlo SE of about 0.009) and the rule picked the
  two-weight model. Script 6 was changed to use the same choosers before its
  results were accepted; the change flipped the recommendation, and the
  researcher accepted it.

3. THE PRIMARY TEST: HOW IT WAS CHOSEN (script 7)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Original rule (fixed before script 7 ran):
    A test qualifies if, in every rule row (no nuisance, each default nuisance
    alone, all defaults together; no effect), at N = 10, 15 and 20, both block
    sizes and both features, its false-positive rate is at most 0.05 + 2 SE
    (that cell's Monte Carlo SE). Among qualifying tests, the one with the
    higher mean power over planning and half shift (all nuisances at their
    defaults; same N, sizes and features) wins; within 0.02 of each other, the
    Wilcoxon test.
  Amendment (made after script 7 ran):
    As the original rule, except that a test qualifies if no more of its rule
    cells exceed 0.05 + 2 SE than chance gives in 95% of runs for a test whose
    false-positive rate is exactly 0.05 (at most 5 of 96 cells).
  Reason:
    The original rule asks every one of 96 cells per test to stay below a limit
    set at 2 Monte Carlo SE. Each cell exceeds that limit with probability about
    0.023 even for a perfectly calibrated test, so about 2.2 exceedances are
    expected by chance alone and an exactly calibrated test fails the rule in
    most runs. In script 7 each test exceeded the limit in exactly one cell, the
    same cell for both (block order, 180 trials, N = 20, contrast), by margins
    below 0.001 (t-test 0.0579 against 0.0576; Wilcoxon 0.0567 against 0.0567),
    with mean false-positive rates of 0.050 (t) and 0.047 (Wilcoxon) over the
    rule rows. The amendment keeps the intent (no test whose false positives are
    inflated beyond chance qualifies) and removes the multiple-comparison
    artefact. It was made after script 7 had run, by the researcher, and is
    recorded here as such.
  Recomputed from script 7's results (96 rule cells per test; chance of one cell over the
  limit for an exactly calibrated test 0.0228; expected 2.2; 95% of runs give at most 5):

7. C1 (script 8)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  C1 compares the report group's derealization-block weights with the
  comparison group's ORDINARY-block weights (as the PI recommended): one-sided
  Mann-Whitney per feature, report group higher, Holm across the two features.
  First, the attention check: the comparison group against the report group on
  catch failures over the session and on the share of responses under 300 ms
  (one-sided Mann-Whitney, Holm); if the comparison group looks less
  attentive, C1 is inconclusive. Secondary check: the two groups'
  ORDINARY-block weights, same test. Comparison group below 20: C1 descriptive
  only.
  Scenario (v4's C1 scenario): half the report group respond (0.5 / 0.7 per
  SD, both features); a quarter of each group pick the more edited-looking
  image whatever the episode; mean lapse 0.1. Script 8's 'realistic' C1 rows
  add script 7's default nuisances, except that script 7's 'edited looks odd'
  quarter (-0.5 on both features, against the more edited image) is replaced
  by v4's C1 strategy: a quarter of each group pick the more heavily
  edited-looking image (+0.5 on both features in every block). The two use the
  same slot of the person model, so they cannot both be on.

8. THE COMPARISON GROUP'S EXPECTATION BLOCK
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The comparison group's expectation block is a side benchmark. It decides
  nothing: it is used only in two exploratory, descriptive analyses, and no
  power simulation was run for them.
    Exploratory 1: Within the comparison group: expectation-block weights
    against the same people's ordinary-block weights (paired, per feature). Asks
    whether people who never had derealization expect it to look faded.
    Exploratory 2: Across groups: the report group's derealization-block weights
    against the comparison group's expectation-block weights (the v4 version of
    C1), reported beside C1.
