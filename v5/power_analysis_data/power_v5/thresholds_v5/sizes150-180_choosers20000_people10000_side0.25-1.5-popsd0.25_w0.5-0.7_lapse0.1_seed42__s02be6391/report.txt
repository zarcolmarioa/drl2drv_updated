6. TRIAL ORDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
(i) The same random choosers answering every item the same way, fitted on
each order's presentation sequence: the largest difference in any person's
weight and in any threshold. (ii) Independent random choosers on each order:
the largest difference from the registered thresholds.
Why the thresholds cannot depend on the order: a random chooser's answers
do not depend on a trial's position, and the fit does not depend on the
order of the rows. Order could matter for real people (fatigue, practice
within a block); that is a question for script 7, not for the thresholds.

7. RECOMMENDATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The rule, fixed before the run: register the side model only if
  (a) at a side bias of 0.5 logit (either direction) the two-weight model's
      false-detection rate exceeds 0.035 in either tail, or a weight moves
      by more than 0.05; AND
  (b) the side model loses at most 0.01 of detection at the planning
      weights in every set, size and feature.
  (a) worst tail rate 0.0354 (180 B, saturation, right 0.5); worst weight shift 0.0565
      (150 B, contrast, planning weights, right 0.5)  ->  (a) HOLDS
  (b) largest detection loss +0.0046 (150 A, saturation)  ->  (b) HOLDS
  For comparison, a population whose side biases vary between people (SD 0.25 logit,
  an assumption): two-weight model, worst tail rate 0.0292, worst weight shift 0.0098.

  Not part of the rule, for both models: worst false-detection rate of random
  choosers (either tail, any set and size) at each fixed side bias (either
  direction), and whether it is lopsided (one tail) or symmetric:
    two-weight: 0: 0.029 (tails differ by up to 0.005); 0.25: 0.033 (tails differ by up to 0.013); 0.5: 0.035 (tails differ by up to 0.024)
      0.75: 0.038 (tails differ by up to 0.029); 1: 0.039 (tails differ by up to 0.034); 1.5: 0.037 (tails differ by up to 0.036)
    side: 0: 0.029 (tails differ by up to 0.005); 0.25: 0.029 (tails differ by up to 0.006); 0.5: 0.032 (tails differ by up to 0.006)
      0.75: 0.037 (tails differ by up to 0.008); 1: 0.043 (tails differ by up to 0.007); 1.5: 0.059 (tails differ by up to 0.008)
  A lopsided rise pushes weights in one direction (a bias); a symmetric rise
  widens the spread (more detections in both directions). Under strong side
  bias the side model's random choosers spread more, because one-sided answers
  carry less information about the features.

  RECOMMENDED: the side model. A side preference of the size in the rule moves
  the two-weight model's weights or false-detection rates beyond the rule's
  limits, and the side model costs at most the detection shown in (b).
  Its thresholds are NOT the ones in design.json: registering it means
  replacing the frozen thresholds with these (same random choosers, side model):
    150 set A: saturation +0.328 / -0.331   contrast +0.516 / -0.521
    150 set B: saturation +0.328 / -0.333   contrast +0.511 / -0.521
    180 set A: saturation +0.301 / -0.305   contrast +0.469 / -0.465
    180 set B: saturation +0.304 / -0.301   contrast +0.452 / -0.462
  The side term is a nuisance term only: its estimate is never reported as a
  result.
