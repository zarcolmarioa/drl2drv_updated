6. THE CHOICE OF PRIMARY TEST
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The rule, fixed before the run: a test qualifies if its false-positive rate is
at most 0.05 + 2 SE (that cell's Monte Carlo SE) in every rule row (section 4), at N = 10, 15, 20, both sizes
and both features. Among qualifying tests, the higher mean power over planning
and half shift (all nuisances at defaults, same N, sizes, features) wins; within
0.02 of each other, the wilcoxon test.
  Cells per test: 96. If a test's true rate were exactly 0.05 everywhere, about
  2.2 of them would exceed the limit by chance alone (up to 5 in 95% of runs), so a test
  that is exactly calibrated can fail this rule by chance; the cells over the limit
  are listed so this can be judged. The rule is applied as fixed; this line is
  information only.

8. SECONDARY: SIGN TEST ON DETECTED COUNTS WITHIN EACH BLOCK
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Per block, each person's weight is classified with the per-set thresholds of
script 6 (side model); the claim needs d >= 5 detections and the exact one-sided
binomial (v4's Table 4 rule). Derealization block: the secondary H1/H2.
Ordinary block: the same rule, as a reference.
Note: within one block the sign test asks whether a directional preference exists;
it cannot tell a preference shared by both episodes (e.g. 'memory makes it duller',
one of the default nuisances) from a derealization effect, so with nuisances at
their defaults it claims in many studies with no derealization effect (second table).
That is why the paired test is primary.
